# CredGate benchmark

Accuracy of credgate's PII detection on a **synthetic, labelled corpus** of
Indian-context documents. The corpus is in this folder
([`corpus/dev.jsonl`](corpus/dev.jsonl), [`corpus/test.jsonl`](corpus/test.jsonl),
700 documents each) and is generated by
[`generate_corpus.py`](generate_corpus.py); every name, identifier and
address in it is synthetic. Results are for the held-out `test` split; the
`dev` split was used to find and fix bugs, so `test` was not tuned against.
The R and Python packages produce identical numbers.

Measured on 2026-11-01 on corpus version 2 (below), with the development
version after Python 0.5.0 (R 0.1.0.9000).

## Summary

At the recommended threshold (`min_confidence = 0.5`):

- **Overall: precision 1.000, recall 0.961, F1 0.980** - no false positives
  in 700 documents.
- 19 of 22 types score F1 1.000, including the newest: card verification
  codes, card expiry dates, ration card and NREGA job card numbers.
- Weakest: **voter ID** (recall 0.51) - a bare `ABC1234567` without a word
  such as "voter" or "EPIC" is reported at 0.3, below the threshold, because
  the format alone could be a product code; **CKYC numbers** (recall 0.67) -
  a bare 14-digit number without a cue such as "CKYC" or "KIN" is not
  reported at all, by design; and **names** (recall 0.93) in running text
  without an honorific, label or "ji".
- Without a threshold, low-confidence matches on order and reference numbers
  that pass the Aadhaar checksum by chance cut Aadhaar precision; they are
  reported (at 0.42-0.48) so callers can decide.

### Corpus version 2 (2026-11-01)

The corpus now includes the identifiers added after 0.5.0 - CKYC, ration
card and NREGA job card numbers, card verification codes and expiry dates -
in KYC forms, card payment notes, call-centre notes and Hinglish chats, a
bare record (where a CKYC number cannot be recognised without a cue), and a
negative document with full dates next to "valid till". Adding templates
changes every generated document, so numbers on version 2 are not directly
comparable with those in the next section, measured on version 1.

### Changes on corpus version 1 (2026-10-31 to 2026-11-01)

Overall F1 at 0.5 rose from 0.947 to 0.983 (precision 0.997 to 0.999)
after four rule changes, each found by studying errors on `dev`:

- the stronger cue wins - a keyword before a match (where labels go) beats
  one after it, and on the same side the nearer one wins: "Aadhaar ... for
  e-KYC; order ..." keeps the Aadhaar (Aadhaar recall 0.80 to 1.00, mobile
  0.89 to 1.00);
- an IFSC next to a number is evidence of a bank account (recall 0.75 to
  0.99), and phone words count against it;
- "ji" after a name is a name cue (name recall 0.84 to 0.92);
- capitalised words such as "Word" or "Navami" after an honorific are not
  names ("Ms Word", "Shri Ram Navami").

**Read the new numbers with care.** `dev` and `test` are generated from the
same templates, so a rule that fixes a `dev` error fixes the same pattern in
`test`. The improvement on `test` therefore overstates the improvement on
text credgate has not seen; an independently written corpus is needed to
measure that.

## Caveats

- **Synthetic data.** Documents are generated from templates (KYC forms,
  bank messages, call-centre notes, Hinglish chats, Hindi text, hospital
  summaries, bare records, and documents with no PII). The numbers describe
  realistic-looking synthetic text, not any real customer data, and real
  text will be messier. No real personal data was used.
- **Templates are known.** Fixes made on `dev` can generalise to `test`
  more easily than to unseen layouts. More template families and an
  independently written corpus are on the roadmap.
- **Home advantage.** We wrote the corpus generator, so its documents suit
  credgate's design. See the comparison below for how Presidio fares on the
  same data, and read it with this in mind.

## How to reproduce

```sh
nix develop
PYTHONPATH=pkg/python/src python benchmark/generate_corpus.py   # regenerates the corpus (deterministic)
PYTHONPATH=pkg/python/src python benchmark/run_benchmark.py test
```

or in R: `cg_evaluate("benchmark/corpus/test.jsonl", min_confidence = 0.5)`.
To evaluate on your own labelled data, use the same JSON Lines format
(see `spec/README.md`, "Evaluation").

## Throughput

How fast credgate processes text on **one CPU core** of a 2019 laptop (Intel
Core i5-8257U, 1.4 GHz); a server core is faster. Inputs are built from the
synthetic corpus by [`throughput.py`](throughput.py) and
[`throughput.R`](throughput.R): `field` is one identifier per value (a
structured column), `note` one corpus document, `long` 12 documents joined
(~900 characters), `clean` the documents without PII. Values per second for
`detect_pii`:

| Input | Python before | Python after | R before | R after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| field (14 characters) | 18,548 | 26,909 | 5,398 | 6,678 |
| note (75 characters) | 5,411 | 5,822 | 1,695 | 2,734 |
| long (913 characters) | 429 | 433 | 152 | 293 |
| clean (71 characters) | 7,439 | 9,248 | 1,905 | 2,203 |

"Before" is 0.4.0; "after" is 0.5.0. `redact()` runs at the same
speed as detection; R's `tokenise()` went from 538 to 2,672 notes per second
(the vault no longer grows one row at a time). `scan_pii()` on 8 columns x
20,000 rows: 4.6 s to 3.0 s in Python.

**What limits it.** About 0.4 MB of text per second per core in Python
(0.2 in R). In Python, about 60% of the time is the regular-expression engine
itself - the name patterns most - and most of the rest is building results.
Detection is independent per text, so it scales with cores and machines:
it runs inside DuckDB, Polars and Arrow pipelines (see
[docs/large-data.md](../docs/large-data.md)); Spark is planned, and a
compiled matching engine (such as PCRE2 with its JIT compiler) is being
considered.

## Proxy throughput

[`proxy_load.py`](proxy_load.py) puts the proxy under parallel load and
checks every answer. Three processes: a fake OpenAI-compatible endpoint that
answers at once by echoing what it received (so the proxy's own cost is
measured, not a model's) and counts identifier-shaped strings in what
reaches it (each one a leak); the proxy (`credgate proxy serve`) with a
policy that tokenises every identifier; and the load generator, whose
clients send synthetic customer records over kept-alive connections. Each
answer must be restored exactly to the original text; after each run the
audit chain is verified. Raw results: [`results/`](results/).

Measured 2026-11-02 on the 2019 laptop above (4 cores, 8 threads), with the
clients and the fake endpoint on the same machine, so the numbers are

**What limits it.** Detection is CPU work: about 0.22 MB of text per second
per worker on this laptop (one core), so a worker handles about 40 requests
of 5 KB per second. Python runs one thread at a time per process, so each
worker takes turns for the policy step (waiting for the model still
overlaps), and more cores need more workers: 4 workers gave 3-3.5x the
throughput of one. Beyond one machine, run instances behind a load balancer
(see [the proxy guide](../docs/proxy.md)). With a real model, its own time
(seconds per answer) dominates; the proxy adds about 4 ms for a short
message and 25 ms for 5 KB of text.

Two findings from these runs went into the proxy: threads competing for
detection made one worker slower under load than alone (17 instead of 38
requests per second at 8 clients), fixed by taking turns; and workers waiting
on the shared socket could miss a shutdown, fixed with an accept timeout.

With one worker the two servers are alike: detection is the limit. With four
workers uvicorn handles 70-80% of the standard server's requests on this
laptop, as each request passes between uvicorn's event loop and a thread.
Streamed answers are slightly faster on uvicorn, which sends whatever pieces
have arrived as one write. With a real model, whose seconds per answer
dominate, the difference does not show; what uvicorn adds is a production
HTTP layer, a limit on requests in progress, and noticing at once a client
that leaves.

Two findings from these runs went into the uvicorn server: handing each
streamed piece to the event loop separately halved streaming throughput (and
the heaviest run timed out), fixed by reading the endpoint in one thread and
sending whatever has queued up together; and reading the request body from
the request's thread cost a second hand-over, fixed by reading it on the
event loop once the client's key checks out.

## BigQuery sampling cost

[`bigquery_tablesample_cost.py`](bigquery_tablesample_cost.py) measures
what BigQuery bills for credgate's sampling queries, on a 3.2 GB synthetic
table (30 million rows) created inside BigQuery. Measured 2026-11-02 in the
BigQuery sandbox, 10,000 rows of one text column:

| Query | Dry-run estimate | Billed |
|---|---:|---:|
| `TABLESAMPLE SYSTEM (1 PERCENT)` | 32.0 MB | 32.5 MB |
| `TABLESAMPLE SYSTEM (10 PERCENT)` | 319.9 MB | 320.9 MB |
| `WHERE RAND() < 0.001` | 3.20 GB | 3.20 GB |

Block sampling bills only the sampled blocks (about 1% of the table at
least), and the dry run estimates it to within about 1 MB, so credgate's
cost check uses the dry run. BigQuery's own `maximumBytesBilled`, however,
checks `TABLESAMPLE` queries against the whole table: with a 200 MB limit
it refused the 1% sample ("3199205376 or higher required"). credgate
therefore does not send that limit with `TABLESAMPLE` queries. Tables
smaller than about 1 GB are a single block, so sampling them reads the
whole table.

## Comparison with Microsoft Presidio

[Presidio](https://github.com/microsoft/presidio) (MIT) is the most widely
used open-source PII detector and ships recognisers for Indian identifiers,
so it is the natural reference. Other tools we were asked about (MaskFlow,
PII Shield, Kavach) are not compared yet: we first need to confirm that each
is a runnable open-source tool whose licence allows publishing results.

### Method

- **Same data, same rules.** The held-out `test` split, scored by the spec's
  evaluation rules; the comparison script's scorer is checked to reproduce
  `credgate.evaluate()` exactly before anything is reported.
- **Thresholds tuned on `dev` for every system** (best overall F1 on the
  types both tools cover), including credgate, then applied to `test`.
- **Two Presidio configurations.** *Default* is `AnalyzerEngine()` as
  installed - it loads **none** of Presidio's Indian recognisers.
  *India-configured* adds all six (Aadhaar, PAN, GSTIN, voter ID, passport,
  vehicle registration) and parses phone numbers with India as a region; it
  is the main comparison.
- **Entity mapping.** `IN_AADHAAR`, `IN_PAN`, `IN_GSTIN`, `IN_VOTER`,
  `IN_PASSPORT`, `EMAIL_ADDRESS`, `PHONE_NUMBER` (mobile), `CREDIT_CARD`
  (card), `PERSON` (name), `LOCATION` (address) and `US_BANK_NUMBER` (bank
  account). Other Presidio entities (dates, URLs, US identifiers, ...) are
  listed separately, not counted as errors.
- **Two views.** *Common types* (types both tools have a recogniser for) is
  the like-for-like comparison; *all 22 types* also counts types Presidio has
  no recogniser for (UPI, IFSC, ABHA, driving licence, PIN code, CKYC,
  ration card, NREGA job card, card verification code and expiry).

Versions: credgate 0.5.0 plus the new detectors; presidio-analyzer 2.2.366, spaCy 3.8.18, en_core_web_lg 3.8.0.

Test split: 700 documents, 1320 labelled spans. Thresholds tuned on dev for best overall F1 on the types both tools cover.

### Reading the results

- **On this Indian-context corpus, credgate scores higher** (F1 0.980 vs
  0.674 on common types, India-configured Presidio). The gaps are genuine, not scoring
  artefacts - exact and overlap matching agree except for names and
  addresses, where Presidio's spans are shorter or include the honorific:
  - *Aadhaar*: Presidio misses every Aadhaar written in Devanagari digits and
    flags order and transaction numbers that pass the checksum (it has no
    negative context such as "order", "txn").
  - *PAN*: it also labels 10-digit mobile numbers as PAN.
  - *Names*: the English NER misses Hindi names and flags phrases such as
    "Mera aadhaar" or "Anna Salai" (a road).
  - *Mobile*: it flags amounts and bank account numbers.
  - *Addresses*: Presidio finds place names, not addresses (`LOCATION`), so
    it scores 0 with exact matching and 0.63 with overlap.
- **Presidio wins on voter IDs without context** (F1 1.000 vs 0.679). Both
  tools report a bare `ABC1234567` at a low score, but Presidio's score
  passes its tuned threshold (0.1) and credgate's 0.3 is below its 0.5. We
  keep 0.3: the format alone could be a product code, and a threshold low
  enough to count these would also count order numbers that pass the Aadhaar
  checksum by chance.
- **Speed**: credgate processed both splits (1,400 documents) in about 0.3
  seconds; Presidio took about 12 seconds, mostly in spaCy's NER (same machine,
  single process).
- **Presidio is general-purpose and extensible.** It can be given custom
  recognisers for UPI, IFSC or ABHA; this comparison is of what it ships.

### Where Presidio is ahead

This benchmark measures one thing - Indian identifiers in synthetic
Indian-context text - and credgate was designed for exactly that. Presidio
is a broader, more mature tool, and is ahead of credgate in several ways:

- **Breadth.** Presidio detects many entity types credgate does not:
  international identifiers (US, UK, EU and others), IBAN, IP and MAC
  addresses, URLs, crypto wallets, dates, medical licences, nationality and
  religion (NRP). credgate is deliberately India-focused and covers none of
  these.
- **Names in running text.** Presidio's statistical NER finds names without
  an honorific, field label or "ji"; credgate does not yet (its name recall
  here comes from cues the corpus contains).
- **Anonymisation options.** Presidio's anonymiser can replace, mask, hash or
  encrypt with a key, with custom operators; credgate redacts, or tokenises
  with a vault that can be saved encrypted (no hashing or encryption operators).
- **Beyond plain text.** Presidio redacts images (OCR) and structured data,
  and ships a REST service and Docker images.
- **Languages and extensibility.** Multiple NLP back-ends (spaCy, Stanza,
  transformers) and languages, context-aware enhancers, and a documented
  recogniser API: the gaps above can be closed in Presidio with custom
  recognisers.
- **Maturity.** Years of production use, a large community and extensive
  documentation; credgate is pre-alpha.
- **This corpus favours credgate.** It is synthetic and generated by us.
  On general English text, or other domains, Presidio may well do better.
